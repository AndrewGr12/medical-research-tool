How your data are handled, and a note about third-party scripts
  • Everything runs in your browser. Your file is read and analyzed on your own computer. No patient data are sent to a server, an analytics service, or any AI model, and this page makes no AI or API calls.
  • External dependencies. This page loads two open-source libraries from public CDNs: Chart.js (charts) and SheetJS (Excel files). Those servers receive a request for the library files (and can see your IP address and browser details, as with any website), but never your data.
  • Optional fully offline use. Download chart.umd.js (Chart.js 4.4.1) and xlsx.full.min.js (SheetJS 0.20.3) into a folder named vendor next to index.html, then change the two <script src="https://..."> lines in index.html to vendor/chart.umd.js and vendor/xlsx.full.min.js. Check with your institution's policy before using any web tool with identifiable patient data; remove direct identifiers where possible.

The tool suggests a type for each column. These are suggestions; please check them. Numeric codes such as 0/1, 1/2/3 or 999 can be categories, identifiers or missing-value codes, not measurements.

Dataset quality summary

Missing values are excluded from the calculations for the corresponding variable only. Nothing is replaced with zero, imputed, or deleted, and no records are removed.

Applying codes re-reads the dataset and re-detects variable types. The codes apply to all columns, so only use values that mean “missing” everywhere.

Choosing between mean (SD) and median (IQR)

The right summary depends on how your data are distributed and on your research question. Mean (SD) is commonly used when values are roughly symmetric; median (IQR) is often preferred when values are skewed or include extreme values. Look at the histogram in step 4 before deciding. This tool does not choose for you.

Explore visualizations and download results

Three basic charts. Pick a variable in each chart's dropdown; charts update right away.

Histogram

A histogram shows how values are distributed. It can help you identify common ranges, skewness, and potential unusual values.

Bar chart

A bar chart compares the number or percentage of observations in each category.

Show

Box plot

A box plot summarizes the median and spread of a continuous variable. When grouped, it helps you compare distributions across categories.

  • Box: the middle half of the values (Q1 to Q3)
  • Thick line inside the box: median
  • Whiskers: the most extreme values within 1.5 × IQR of the box
  • Hollow circles: potential outliers beyond the whiskers

Potential outliers are not automatically errors. They may be real, clinically meaningful values. Investigate them before deciding whether to correct or remove anything.
